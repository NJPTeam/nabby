Nabby vs yt-dlp (CLI)

The engine versus its wrapper. When each one is the right answer.

The short version

yt-dlp is the command-line engine. It is more flexible than any UI, including Nabby. Nabby is a web form that calls yt-dlp. It exists so you don't have to open a terminal or remember flags.

Choose yt-dlp CLI if…

  • You're scripting bulk downloads, archiving channels, or integrating with other tools.
  • You need obscure flags: format selection by codec, remuxing, post-processors, cookie files, custom headers.
  • You're comfortable in a terminal and don't want to run a web server.
  • You want the maximum possible flexibility.

Choose Nabby if…

  • You want to send a link to a non-technical friend or family member to download themselves.
  • You want to grab something from your phone's browser without SSHing anywhere.
  • You have "future you in two years who forgot every flag" in mind.
  • You want a UI with a format picker, an audio-only toggle, and a progress bar — without maintaining a desktop app.

Honest notes

Every Nabby user is also a potential yt-dlp CLI user. We won't pretend otherwise. If you know your way around yt-dlp -f "bv*[height<=1080]+ba/b" --write-sub --sub-langs en.*, the CLI is strictly more powerful. Nabby's whole value proposition is removing that cognitive load for the 90% of downloads where it isn't needed.
