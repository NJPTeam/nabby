Nabby vs MeTube

Both wrap yt-dlp. They want very different things from your box.

The short version

MeTube is a popular self-hosted yt-dlp frontend focused on queueing downloads to a persistent archive. Nabby is a self-hosted yt-dlp frontend focused on grabbing a file and getting out — no queue, no archive, no state.

Choose MeTube if…

  • You want a queue that persists across restarts and a visible history of what you've downloaded.
  • You're building a long-running local archive and want files to accumulate in a folder you manage.
  • You need YouTube support out of the box.
  • You want the most battle-tested self-hosted yt-dlp UI with years of community use.

Choose Nabby if…

  • You want the lightest possible UI — paste, pick, download, leave no trace on the server.
  • You deliberately don't want a queue, history, or local archive — this is just a convenience layer over yt-dlp.
  • You want files auto-deleted after use for privacy or disk-space reasons.
  • You want the "no YouTube" policy baked in for legal cleanliness on a public-facing instance.

Honest notes

MeTube is excellent and more feature-complete today. Nabby trades features for simplicity: there is no job history page, no bulk-import tools, no file manager. If you need any of those, MeTube is the right answer. If what you want is "a web form that calls yt-dlp and hands me the file," Nabby is deliberately smaller.
